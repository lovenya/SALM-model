● I've read through the SpeechAudio LM proposal. Here's my understanding of the
  project:

  Goal: A bidirectional generative model mapping between environmental audio and
  speech — given ambient audio (e.g., rainfall, traffic, fire), generate
  plausible speech that fits the context, and vice versa. It's meant to go
  beyond standard TTS or audio captioning by requiring joint reasoning over
  audio context + speech content.

  Key pieces:
  - Dataset: paired (audio environment ↔ speech) samples — synthetic scenes +
  LLM-generated plausible utterances + TTS synthesis, referred to as the ttas
  dataset (already linked/complete per the doc).
  - Methodology: autoregressive model (VALL-E style) over discrete audio tokens
  (EnCodec/WavTokenizer), trained on 4 tasks — Speech→Audio, Audio→Speech,
  Text→Speech (warmup), Text→Audio (warmup) — with cross-entropy loss + possible
  auxiliary losses (WER, sound classification). Also exploring flow
  matching/diffusion as continuous alternatives, possibly hybrid (continuous in,
  discrete out).
  - Baselines: AR w/ delay pattern (AudioGen), VALL-E (SpeechBrain impl),
  diffusion/flow-matching (multi-band diffusion).
  - Evaluation: mix of human eval, semantic similarity (transcription similarity
  vs. multiple reference speech samples), model-judge approaches (SALMon-style
  background consistency/alignment, SpeechQA models like Qwen), objective
  metrics (FAD, KLD, CLAP score, ViSQOL for audio; UTMOS for speech), plus
  zero-shot generalization to unseen audio environments.

  I understand this repo (SALM-model) is where you've made progress on data and
  metrics/evals pieces of this proposal. Ready when you want to walk me through
  it.

● Answering your four questions directly:

  1. Encoder, not transcriber — correct. I mean WhisperModel.encoder only:
  waveform → hidden states (for large-v3, ~1500×1280). The decoder is never
  loaded, no text is ever produced. That distinction matters for the paper —
  going through a transcript is exactly what your cascaded baseline does, so an
  end-to-end model must not.

  2. Frozen MLLM is very doable — and it isn't a fork in the road. The trick:
  since the encoder is frozen, precompute its features once to disk, then
  training only touches the mapper (+U-Net). So we precompute both Whisper and
  Qwen2.5-Omni features and pick later based on results, instead of betting now.
  Qwen2.5-Omni-3B is already local at models/qwen2.5-omni-3b, and Audio-Omni's
  exact recipe is readable at
  code/Audio-Omni/audio_omni/models/conditioners.py:39-288 (thinker hidden
  states at layer_idx=-2, zero-init linear adapter) — so we're copying a
  known-working pattern, not inventing one. Precompute for 15k files: Whisper
  ~10 min, Qwen ~40 min sharded across 4 GPUs.

  3. Frozen AudioLDM — only in Rung 2. In Rung 3 the U-Net is finetuned (that's
  what makes it AudioLDM's actual recipe). What stays frozen always: mel VAE,
  HiFi-GAN vocoder, CLAP, and the speech encoder. Trainable: mapper + U-Net.

  4. Rung 3 directly — yes, with one exception I'd push back on. Keep Rung 0 as
  a ~20-minute gate first. It's pure inference, no training, and it proves the
  weights load offline, the VAE decodes, and the vocoder makes audible sound.
  Skipping it risks discovering a broken generation path after burning 3 hours
  of training. Rung 1/2 become optional controls on spare GPUs. So: Rung 0 gate 
  → Rung 3 main run (DDP, 4×H100).

❯ what is mel round-trip ocrrelation                                            

● Mel round-trip correlation — concretely, the gate does this:

  1. Take a real ambient clip from the dataset
  2. Compute its log-mel with my extractor → mel_A
  3. Feed mel_A to AudioLDM's HiFi-GAN vocoder → waveform
  4. Recompute the log-mel of that waveform → mel_B
  5. Pearson correlation between mel_A and mel_B

  The vocoder was trained to invert exactly AudioLDM's mel representation. So if
  my mel matches, the vocoder reconstructs the clip faithfully and correlation
  lands ~0.95+. If my numeric range is wrong, the vocoder emits noise and
  correlation collapses toward 0. It deliberately skips the VAE, so a failure
  points at the mel and nothing else. Correlation rather than MSE because it's
  scale-invariant and reads as one interpretable 0–1 number.

  The 512-d vector is a per-sample activation — the conditioning signal handed
  to the U-Net — not weights. The mapper's job is to produce it; the mapper
  itself is 32.3 M weights (a 1280→1024 projection, two transformer layers over
  the Whisper frames, attention pooling, then a head down to 512).

  Worth flagging, because it's a genuine scientific point for your meeting: that
  512-d vector is a hard information bottleneck. Everything the model knows
  about the utterance — "someone is angry about a near-miss in traffic" — has to
  pass through 512 floats, because that's the only conditioning channel
  AudioLDM's U-Net exposes (class_embed_type=simple_projection). That's fine for
  scene-level semantics, which is what this task needs, but it structurally
  cannot carry fine-grained detail, and it caps how much the mapper can ever
  convey.

  If results are weak and you want more conditioning bandwidth later, that's the
  architectural limit to attack — e.g. AudioLDM 2, which conditions via
  cross-attention over a sequence rather than one pooled vector. Worth having
  that answer ready if your supervisor asks "why only 512 dimensions?" — it's
  AudioLDM's constraint, not our choice.

  That split is deliberate. 15k pairs cannot train a latent diffusion model, a
  mel VAE, or a vocoder from scratch — so the entire generative path is
  borrowed, and we only learn the new thing: how to map speech semantics into
  the conditioning space AudioLDM already understands. The U-Net gets a
  deliberately low LR (1e-5 vs the mapper's 1e-4) precisely so finetuning adapts
  it without erasing the audio prior we're relying on.

  One risk worth being upfront about, since it follows from these numbers: 32.3 
  M from-scratch params on 15,033 samples is ~2,150 params per training example.
  That's a lot of capacity for that much data, and overfitting is the likely
  failure mode, not underfitting. What's guarding against it: dropout 0.1,
  weight decay 1e-2, cosine LR decay, and checkpoint_best.pt selected on val
  loss rather than final weights. If the loss curve shows train falling while
  val flattens or rises early, the fix is a smaller mapper (1 layer, hidden 512)
  rather than more training — that's the first knob I'd turn on run 2.

  Incidental note in case it comes up: Audio-Omni zero-initializes its adapter
  so the frozen generator's behavior is preserved at step 0 and the model learns
  to deviate. We can't copy that directly, because the mapper L2-normalizes its
  output and a zero vector would give 0/0. A gentler start would need a
  different trick (e.g. blending toward the learned null embedding early).

  features natively with no pooling loss. Costs: more moving parts, an extra

  What a U-Net is, and your figure question

  Your reading of the figure is right, but the two arrows have completely 
  different roles:

  - VAE encoder → latent diffusion: this is the data. The mel becomes a latent z
  (8×256×16), which is what gets noised and then denoised. It's what you're 
  generating.
  - Audio/text encoder → latent diffusion: this is the condition — one 512-d
  vector. It's what tells the model what to generate. It's never denoised.

  Think of it as: the latent is the canvas, the condition is the instruction.

  The U-Net is the network that does the denoising. Its job: given a noisy
  latent plus a timestep, predict the noise that was added. Named for its shape:

  input latent (8×256×16)
     ↓ downsample  ──────────skip──────────┐
     ↓ downsample  ────skip────┐           │
     ↓ downsample              │           │
        [bottleneck]           │           │
     ↑ upsample                │           │
     ↑ upsample    ←───────────┘           │
     ↑ upsample    ←───────────────────────┘
  output: predicted noise (8×256×16)

  Why this shape:
  - Output must match input dimensions (it predicts a noise tensor of the same
  shape), which the symmetric down-then-up path gives you.
  - Downsampling builds global context — "this is a traffic scene."
  - Skip connections carry fine detail straight across, which downsampling would
  otherwise destroy — needed for crisp audio texture.

  Where the condition enters: in AudioLDM 1, the 512-d vector is projected and
  added to the timestep embedding, which is fed into every block
  (class_embed_type=simple_projection). So it modulates the whole network
  globally rather than attending to specific positions. That's precisely why
  it's one pooled vector and why the 512-d bottleneck exists — and it's the
