03 / Model

From context to waveform.

Generate a plausible soundscape from situated speech, or plausible speech from an acoustic scene.

Both directions use an AudioLDM latent-diffusion backbone. Speech-to-audio learns a global condition from speech; audio-to-speech adds an acoustic content LM to plan the speech before rendering it. Each direction has its own learned conditioner and denoiser.

01 / Inference

Two directions, two conditioning paths.

At inference, the model receives a source waveform and samples a target waveform. The paired reference recording, caption and transcript are not supplied to the generation path. Starting from Gaussian noise, the U-Net iteratively denoises the target latent using the source condition.

Speech → audio

Read the speech acoustically.

The frozen Whisper large-v3 encoder produces speech features. A learned Transformer conditioner pools them into a 512-dimensional vector that conditions the AudioLDM U-Net. This path uses encoder features directly, without transcribing the speech.

Audio → speech

Plan the speech, then render it.

The frozen CLAP audio encoder supplies scene frames to a global conditioner and to the content LM. The LM predicts acoustic code IDs and durations. A fixed codebook recovers a content feature sequence, which is projected into frame-aligned channels for the speech U-Net.

Latent geometry and conditioning

The AudioLDM-S backbone uses a 10.24-second output canvas. Its latent has 8 channels × 256 time positions × 16 frequency cells; each time position spans 40 ms. The VAE decoder produces a mel spectrogram and the vocoder produces the waveform.

The global condition enters AudioLDM through its class-conditioning embedding. For A2S, a learned 512 → 128 content projection at each time position is reshaped into 8 × 16 cells. Concatenating these eight content channels with the eight noisy-latent channels gives a 16-channel U-Net input.

Both pooled conditioners use a two-layer Transformer followed by attention pooling. The environmental-audio tower is LAION-CLAP HTSAT, using the unfused model. The diffusion backbone is cvssp/audioldm-s-full-v2.

02 / Learning

Paired supervision, staged training.

Training uses the paired target to teach denoising and conditioning. For A2S, the renderer first learns with content extracted from the target speech; the content LM is trained separately to predict that content from environmental audio.

  1. 01 / Prepare speech content

    Learn a representation.

    Train a small speech encoder with character-level CTC and the dataset transcripts. Freeze its encoder, fit a k-means codebook to its hidden features, then create acoustic code and duration targets.

  2. 02 / Train the renderer

    Learn waveform generation.

    Optimize the conditioner and AudioLDM U-Net with denoising MSE and cosine alignment. A2S also learns the content projector while receiving continuous oracle CTC features from the target speech.

  3. 03 / Train the content LM

    Predict content from the scene.

    Train the flat or hierarchical LM with teacher-forced code and duration cross-entropy. The codebook and renderer stay fixed; this stage does not backpropagate through waveform synthesis.

Objectives and the training–inference handoff
Diffusion renderers
Noise-prediction MSE plus cosine alignment, with alignment weight 1. S2A aligns the source condition to the target environmental audio's CLAP embedding. A2S aligns the scene condition to the paired transcript's CLAP text embedding.
Content LM
Next-code cross-entropy plus duration cross-entropy, with duration weight 1. The hierarchical code loss covers the coarse prefix, separator, fine codes and EOS; duration supervision applies only to the fine codes.
Frozen components
Whisper, CLAP, the AudioLDM VAE and vocoder remain fixed. After speech-content preparation, the CTC encoder and codebook remain fixed too. The renderer is fixed during content-LM training.
Content supplied to A2S
Renderer training uses continuous features from the target speech. Inference uses codebook centroids selected by the content LM, expanded onto the same 256-frame grid. This is a training–inference difference; target speech is not read during generation.

03 / Acoustic content

A sequence model for speech units.

“Content” here means acoustic hidden-state codes, rather than words, characters or a readable transcript. The flat and hierarchical variants share the same codebook, duration representation and speech renderer.

Flat · E2E base

Predict fine pairs directly.

Starting from BOS, the model predicts a code and its duration at each step. Adjacent repeated frame IDs are compressed into runs; duration expansion restores their timing before centroid lookup.

Hierarchical · E2E hier.

Generate a coarse prefix first.

Training takes every 12th fine token to build a coarse sketch. At inference, the same LM generates that sketch, a separator, and then the fine code–duration sequence. The fine pass attends to the earlier coarse tokens.

Vocabulary, duration and stopping

The codebook contains 2,048 centroids, each 512-dimensional. It is a single k-means vocabulary, not residual vector quantization. Both LMs use a six-layer causal Transformer decoder, width 512 and eight attention heads, with separate code and duration prediction heads.

The CTC speech encoder uses two strided convolution blocks and a two-layer bidirectional GRU. Content extraction keeps its 512-dimensional hidden states and bypasses the character prediction head.

Input embeddings sum the previous code, previous duration and token position. Scene frames are projected from 768 to 512 dimensions and used as cross-attention memory. Durations range from 1 to 32 frames; longer repeated runs are split when preparing targets.

Generation stops at EOS or the frame budget. Fine pairs expand to the 256-frame canvas; if the predicted sequence ends early, the last code is held for the remaining positions. Coarse tokens and the separator have no duration target and are never rendered.

04 / Baseline

The text-mediated comparison.

The cascades use pretrained components to pass through readable text intermediates. They are the comparison systems alongside the direct models on the examples page.

Audio → speech

Caption → utterance → speech

AF-Next (Audio Flamingo 3) captions the scene; Llama 3.3 70B Instruct proposes a plausible utterance; CosyVoice 3 synthesizes speech.

Speech → audio

Transcript → scene → audio

Whisper large-v3 transcribes speech; Llama 3.3 70B Instruct proposes a scene description; Audio-Omni generates environmental audio.
